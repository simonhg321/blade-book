Why not a spreadsheet

You could type it all out.
Or take one picture.

Everything a collector wants to know about a knife is already written on the card that came in the box. So we read the card.

The old way

  1. Open the laptop. Find the spreadsheet.
  2. Dig out the box. Squint at the card.
  3. Type model, steel, date, inlay, graphic…
  4. Take a photo anyway. Lose track of which is which.
  5. Repeat for every knife you own.

Somewhere past twenty knives

You stop remembering.

Which one was that?

Two Large 21s with the same inlay, born three years apart. The cards know. Do you?

What did I pay?

Price, who you got it from, when. Private to you, attached to the knife, there when you trade.

Where did it go?

Traded, sold, consigned. The record stays, so the story of the knife doesn't end with you.

Two sides to every register

Show what you want.
Keep the rest to yourself.

The private side

Only you, signed in
  • Every knife you own, including the ones nobody knows about
  • What you paid, who from, where it's kept
  • Your own notes and condition calls
  • Any knife can be fully private — no page, no search, no trace

The public side

Your page, your rules
  • A page per knife you choose to show
  • One register page for the collection — a link for your forum signature
  • Mark a knife for sale or trade when you're ready
  • Photos are watermarked with your handle

Plenty of collectors would rather nobody knew what's in the safe. That's the default we respect: nothing is public until you say so, and you can hide the day a knife was born if the card gives too much away.

And then, the good part

Show them off.

The card photo makes the record. Add a second shot — knife open, good light — and that's the one your page leads with.
