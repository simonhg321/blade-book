3 · the marks

Four ways to mark the knife.

Same crop each time. Tap any one for full size — judge them big, that is how a scammer would lift them.

The card shot carries the heavy mark.

The full card shot is what a scammer wants: knife, card and kit in one frame. It sits lower on the page now, so it can carry more.

The honest part. No watermark stops a determined thief; tools erase them in seconds now. A mark stops the lazy one. What actually protects a buyer is the page: “ask the seller for their blade-book link” is a check a scammer cannot pass. The plate teaches that habit every time a photo travels.

4 · yours to call

Open questions

  1. Which mark?A, B, C or D — or a mix, such as plate only on the knife and the etch on the card shot.
  2. Who finds the knife in the photo?We propose a box and the owner nudges it, or the owner draws it. We never pick a face for them.
  3. The name: graphic first, or model first?“Lunar Landing · Large Sebenza 31” or “Large Sebenza 31 — Lunar Landing”.
  4. Does the card shot stay public by default?It is the proof, and it is also the scammer’s prize. It could show only to signed-in collectors.
  5. Flip order.Next and previous follow the shelf: by maker, by birth date, or the owner’s own order.
